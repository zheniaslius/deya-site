01 How it works

Three sources instead of one satellite

An ordinary navigator goes blind as soon as GPS disappears, or insists you are at an airport. Deya keeps guiding you because it knows how the car moves and which roads it can be on.

01 / Sensors

Motion sensors

The phone’s gyroscope and accelerometer track every turn, acceleration and stop.

02 / Camera · optional

Rear camera

Watches the road ahead and measures speed. Frames are analysed in memory and disappear right away. Deya works without the camera too, just with possibly lower accuracy.

03 / Map

Offline map

OpenStreetMap roads are stored on the phone. Your position always snaps to a real road.

04 Privacy

The camera looks at the road, not the internet.

  • 01Frames stay in memoryThe camera lets Deya measure speed without GPS. Frames are analysed in the phone’s memory and disappear right away.
  • 02Photos and video are never sent anywhereUnless you turn on the “Find start place with Gemini” option yourself. Then, if GPS is already jammed at the start, one small picture of the road is sent to Google Gemini. Gemini only reads the text on it: shop signs and street names. The phone itself matches them against the map, and the picture is not stored anywhere.
  • 03No accounts or adsThe app has no accounts, ads or analytics.
  • 04Offline while drivingPositioning and navigation while driving work without internet.

05 Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need internet?

Only to download the map of your city (and, optionally, to refine your start area with Gemini if you turn that option on yourself). After that Deya works out where you are and plans routes completely offline.

How to set up navigation without internet →

Which phone do I need?

An Android 8.0 or newer smartphone with a rear camera, a gyroscope and an accelerometer. Almost all modern phones have them.

How accurate is it without GPS?

In a city with plenty of turns, Deya usually keeps you on the right street. On long straight roads without turns the error slowly grows, and it is corrected again as soon as you reach a turn or real GPS returns. If Deya gets lost, tap the marker button and show it on the map where you are.

What if GPS is already jammed when I start?

Deya takes an approximate place from Wi-Fi or the mobile network, then works out where you are from the shape of your path: the turns you have driven are matched against the streets on the map. The arrow usually snaps to the right street within 300 to 600 metres of driving, and fastest when the camera is on.

Does it drain the battery?

The camera and sensors run for the whole trip, so on long drives keep the phone on a charger.

Why “Deya”?

It comes from the meme about a sheep asking “Where am I?” (“Де я?” in Ukrainian). Now it has an answer.
