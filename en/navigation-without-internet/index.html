Navigation without internet: how not to get lost when the signal drops

Mobile internet disappears during power cuts, on highways between cities and when the network is overloaded. The good news: you do not need internet to see yourself on a map. You only need to prepare your phone in advance.

Updated 6 November 2026 · 5 min read

Does GPS work without internet?

Yes. Your phone receives signals from GPS, Galileo or GLONASS satellites directly, without mobile coverage. Internet only helps find the satellites faster at the start (this is called A-GPS). Without it the first position may take a minute or two instead of a few seconds, especially if the phone has not used GPS for a while. After that everything works as usual.

The problem is something else: most navigators download the map itself, address search and the route from the internet. Without a connection you see a blue dot on a grey field.

What you need for offline navigation

  • A map on the phone. Streets, names and house numbers must be stored in memory, not loaded while you drive.
  • Offline routing. The app must plan and re-plan the route itself, without a server.
  • Offline address search. Otherwise, without a connection, you cannot find where to go.

How to prepare your phone in advance

  1. Download the map of your city and the places you are going to while you have Wi-Fi. A city map takes tens of megabytes.
  2. Test it in airplane mode. Switch it on, find an address and plan a route. If everything works, you are ready.
  3. Turn GPS on before you set off and give the phone a minute to see the sky so a position can appear.
  4. Keep the phone on a charger. Navigating with the screen on drains the battery quickly.
  5. Update your maps every few months so new roads and traffic changes are included.

Which apps work without internet

Google Maps lets you save an offline area and plan a driving route inside it, but you have to do that in advance, and some features are unavailable without a connection. Organic Maps and OsmAnd are built on OpenStreetMap data and work fully offline from the start.

All of them rely on GPS. So when the signal nearby is jammed or spoofed, which happens more and more often in Ukraine, the arrow freezes or jumps kilometres away, even if the map is saved.

When you have neither internet nor GPS

Deya is built for exactly this. It works offline with a map of your city and keeps you on the road when GPS disappears or lies. Deya tracks the car’s movement with the phone’s sensors and rear camera and matches it against the streets on the map. Internet is needed only once, to download your city. Address search and routing keep working without a connection.

Deya is free, runs on Android 8.0 and newer, and does not send camera images anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Does GPS use mobile data?

No. Satellite signals are free and received directly. Only the app uses data, if it downloads maps or routes from the internet.

Can I use navigation in airplane mode?

On most Android phones GPS keeps working in airplane mode. If airplane mode switches it off, turn location back on manually afterwards.

What if GPS shows the wrong place?

Most likely the signal is being jammed or spoofed. Ordinary navigators are helpless here because they trust GPS completely. Deya spots the spoofing and keeps guiding you with sensors, the camera and the map.
